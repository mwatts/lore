lore-storage: Make index changes durable by journal, not bucket rewrites

A flush made each changed index entry durable by rewriting its whole
bucket file through a temporary file, a sync, a rename, and a directory
sync. A small revision touches a dozen or more immutable buckets and a
handful of mutable ones, so a flush cost that many rewrites whatever
the entries changed.

A journaled store now appends the entries changed since the last flush
to its journal, beside the payloads, in the same write and the same
sync. The immutable store records the hashes `store` and `copy` change
and journals their entries as they are at the flush; the mutable store
records the keys `store` and `compare_and_swap` change. Buckets are
written only in a fold: when the journal passes its fold size, when a
change the journal does not record (an eviction, a compaction, an
obliteration) asks for one, before a compaction drops a packfile, and
when the store opens, which first replays what a crash left. Each fold
empties the journal and no bucket is written between folds, so no
record is older than the bucket it lands in, and a replay is
idempotent. A store opened without the journal setting still folds a
journal it finds. Client repository mutable stores turn the journal on.

A flush that changes more than `JOURNAL_BULK_ENTRY_BYTES` of entries
(a revision of hundreds of objects) is journaled like any other and
also writes its buckets, as a flush without the journal does: it
touches most buckets anyway, and journaling it alone only moved those
rewrites into a later fold.

The crash test now journals the mutable store too, and checks its keys
after the reopen.
